Bees are the ultimate architects and engineers of the natural world, and their attendance to the hive is a complex, highly coordinated symphony of labor. Here is how they manage their home and what that famous "hum" actually sounds like.

### How Bees Attend to Their Hive

The hive is not just a storage unit; it is a living, breathing super-organism. Every bee has a specific role, often changing as they age, but the collective goal is always the survival of the colony.

**1. The Architects (Wax Builders)**
Younger bees produce wax from glands on their abdomens. They chew this wax and use it to build the hexagonal honeycomb. The hexagon is nature's most efficient shape, allowing them to store the maximum amount of honey and raise the most brood with the least amount of wax. They constantly repair and expand the comb as the colony grows.
